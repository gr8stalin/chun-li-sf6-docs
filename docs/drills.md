Several players have developed training mode drills in order to compartmentalize reactions to their opponent's actions in specific situations. Drills work by setting the training dummy to do a handful of actions on block and on wakeup and practicing *intentionally* recognizing the opponent's reaction or state, then applying the counter.

This is somewhat universal across fighting games, but Street Fighter 6 is a volatile game that requires consistent effort for any given player to achieve a semblance of stability.

1. Fundamentals
{:toc}

## Fundamentals

These drills are focused on both core SF6 interactions and traditional SF interactions.

### Checking drive rush and jumpins after pokes
<br/>
Set the dummy's block setting to "block all".

Record the dummy doing either empty drive rush or drive rush light, then set this recording as a "reversal after block" action.

Set another "reversal after block" action to forward jump.

Poke the training dummy with 6MP, 2MK, or 5HP. After the dummy blocks the normal, visually confirm their response:

### Checking corner escapes
<br/>
This drill is sourced from one of Zaferino's Sub Sunday sessions. The player in question is using Elena, but she and Chun have sufficient overlap in terms of their buttons.

As Chun, use 6MP and 2MK in place of Elena's 5HK and 2MK, and you can practice reacting to the escape attempt with:

- 22K
- j.MP xx j.236K
- Air throw

When working on this drill, build up to the final step. Zaferino says that when you're actually playing people, you'll clip them with buttons and you can adjust yourself accordingly, but the focus with this drill is accurately responding to the jumpout attempt by the training dummy.

The final step in this drill is incorporating whiff punishes:

{% include twitchEmbed.html id='OnerousSnappyGrassPeoplesChamp-Vw3svd-TFW8dj4t_' %}

With Chun, you can *and should* start to incorporate 5HP here, but 6MP is very sufficient if not as strong of a starter damage-wise. The goal of this drill is strengthening your reactions to escapes and aggression when you have someone cornered: while Chun doesn't have a throw loop, she excels at containment.
